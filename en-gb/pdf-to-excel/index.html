Loona PDF to Excel Converter

In short: this tool turns the tables inside a text-based PDF into a real Excel (.xlsx) workbook — one worksheet per page — built entirely in your browser. It works best on clean, aligned tables; merged cells and multi-line cells come out approximate, and scanned image PDFs cannot be converted because they have no text to read. You preview and can fix every cell before downloading, and your file never leaves your device.

Reports, invoices and statements lock their tables inside PDFs. This converter reads the text layer of your PDF, rebuilds the rows and columns from the position of every word, and hands you an editable preview grid — correct anything, then download a real .xlsx where clean numbers are real Excel numbers, ready to sum, sort and filter.

How it works, step by step

  1. Choose or drop your PDF file.
  2. Press Extract tables — each page becomes an editable preview grid, labelled Page 1, Page 2 and so on.
  3. Fix any cell directly in the preview, then press Download .xlsx. Each page becomes its own worksheet, named Page 1, Page 2 and so on.

Features

  • Preview-and-fix grid: every extracted page is shown as an editable table, so you correct mistakes before the file is built — not after.
  • One worksheet per PDF page, in page order.
  • Clean numbers become real Excel numbers (thousands commas are understood); percentages, dates and currency-prefixed text stay text, exactly as printed.
  • Honest about scans: if a page has no text layer, the tool says so instead of producing a fake empty spreadsheet.
  • Unlimited and free, with no sign-up, no watermark and no upload — the file never leaves your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my file uploaded?

No. The PDF is read and the Excel file is built entirely in your browser — nothing is sent to a server, nothing is stored, and we never see your file.

Which PDFs work best?

Clean, aligned tables in text-based PDFs — the kind exported from Excel, Word, accounting software or a report generator. The tool reads the PDF's text layer and rebuilds rows and columns from the position of every word.

Can it convert a scanned PDF?

No — and it will tell you so honestly. A scanned PDF is a photograph of a page with no text layer, so there is no table to extract. You would need OCR software first; this tool never invents data it cannot read.

What happens with merged cells and multi-line cells?

They come out approximate. Merged cells are not detected as merged, and a cell whose text wraps over two lines may land in two rows. That is exactly why every page is shown as an editable preview grid first — fix any cell, then download.

Do numbers stay numbers in Excel?

Yes, where it is honest to do so. A cell that is a clean number — digits with optional thousands commas and one decimal point — is written as a real Excel number you can sum and sort. Percentages, dates and currency-prefixed text stay as text, exactly as printed in the PDF.

Under the hood

The PDF is opened by PDF.js and its text items — every word with its exact position — are grouped into rows by their vertical position and into columns by clustering their horizontal starting points. The workbook is then written by SheetJS. Both libraries are loaded from this site's own library folder — nothing is fetched from a CDN while you work, and nothing leaves your device. It is a faithful table extraction, not magic: layout tricks a PDF can hide, such as merged cells, are rebuilt approximately and the preview grid exists so you can repair them in seconds.

Worked example

A two-page supplier statement becomes a workbook with two worksheets — Page 1 and Page 2 — each holding that page's table, with quantities and totals as real numbers you can sum straight away in Excel.
