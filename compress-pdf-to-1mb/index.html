Compress PDF to 1 MB

In short: this tool compresses a PDF to 1 MB or under — lossless first, and only as much image compression as the target truly needs.

One MB is the most common single-file upload cap on the web — contact forms, CMS libraries and application portals repeat it everywhere.

Private by design: your files are processed 100% in your browser with JavaScript. They are never uploaded to a server, never stored and never seen by us - close the tab and everything is gone.

How it works, step by step

  1. Choose your PDF — its current size appears immediately, with a note if it already fits under 1 MB.
  2. Press “Compress to 1 MB”. The lossless step runs first; the progress bar shows each render attempt if more is needed.
  3. Download the result and check the reported size before uploading it to your portal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my PDF be exactly 1 MB?

No — at or under 1 MB. Portals and mail servers set maximums, not exact sizes, so the tool stops at the first setting that fits instead of degrading quality further to hit a round number.

Does the text stay selectable?

Only if the lossless first step fits — the result message tells you which path was taken. If the tool had to rebuild pages as images to reach the target, the text becomes part of the pictures (exactly like the Strong mode of the main compressor).

What if my PDF cannot reach 1 MB?

The tool downloads the smallest version it could make and says so honestly, with the achieved size. Very long or photo-dense documents sometimes cannot go that small legibly — splitting them is the practical fix.

My PDF is already under the target. What happens?

The tool tells you it already fits and offers the lossless re-save only. There is no reason to damage a file that already passes.

Is my document uploaded?

No. Both steps run in your browser with pdf-lib and PDF.js — the same libraries as the main Loona compressor. Nothing leaves your device.

Under the hood

First the tool re-saves your PDF with pdf-lib's object streams — a purely structural, lossless repack. If that fits 1 MB, you are done with text still selectable. If not, it renders each page with PDF.js down a ladder of scale/quality settings (from 2× at 90% JPEG down to 0.35× at 30%) and stops at the first setting that fits — never degrading further than your target requires. The status line always reports the true final size and which path was taken.

Worked example

A 22-page scanned thesis chapter at 9 MB reaches about 0.9 MB at 1.5× — figures and equations stay sharp.
